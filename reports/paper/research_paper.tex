\begin{abstract}
Standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and dense vector search frameworks rely on unconstrained Euclidean or cosine text embeddings that fail catastrophically when querying continuous physical systems. In environments governed by non-linear conservation laws, linguistic proximity precipitates the \textit{Semantic-Physical Disconnect}: models retrieve states that share superficial textual descriptions but differ fundamentally in dimensional scale, phase boundaries, or non-equilibrium thresholds. Furthermore, database querying encounters a dual failure mode: relational queries suffer from \textit{chronological blindness} (defaulting to calm pre-storm onset hours rather than dynamic apexes), whereas unconstrained metric vector search suffers from \textit{cross-decadal manifold degeneracy} (matching scale-invariant dimensionless numbers across disparate epochs and losing synoptic meteorological context). Moreover, standard surrogate models output brittle point predictions lacking distribution-free uncertainty guarantees, leaving downstream foundation models susceptible to hallucinated certainty during acute physical shocks.

To resolve these failure modes, we propose the \textbf{Two-Tier Hybrid Cascade Physics-RAG} architecture augmented with \textbf{Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR)}. Our framework projects heterogeneous multi-modal telemetries into a scale-invariant, 14-dimensional physical manifold derived from first-principles conservation laws and dimensional similitude. Retrieval is executed through a two-tier cascade: Tier~1 performs sub-15-millisecond zero-copy columnar relational pre-filtering via DuckDB to enforce spatial, temporal, and hard boundary constraints, while Tier~2 executes non-Euclidean manifold ranking governed by an asymmetric \textit{Directional Pareto} objective that prioritizes physical disequilibrium excess over symmetric geometric proximity. To combat Markovian memorylessness, the architecture hydrates continuous parent sequences ($\pm 72\text{ hours}$). Crucially, we integrate a distribution-free Conformal Prediction engine calibrated via multi-site stratified chronological splits, generating finite-sample valid prediction intervals that dynamically expand and contract with hydrograph volatility.

We evaluate this architecture across an empirical proving ground of extreme coastal morphodynamics spanning 175,392 hourly records and 1,228 in-situ field surveys across four contrasting global archetypes. On a comprehensive $N = 100$ query benchmark partitioned into three operational tiers and four modes (including deliberate ambiguity stress tests and 3D physical pathologies), the Hybrid Cascade maintains state-of-the-art retrieval competency ($\text{Precision@5} = 0.480$, $\text{NDCG@5} = 0.522$, $\text{MRR} = 0.498$, $\text{Regime Purity} = 0.522$), achieving statistically significant superiority over Pure Physics-RAG ($+0.0560$, $p = 0.0253$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[+0.0020, +0.1120]$) and substantial ranking gains over Rule-Based SQL ($+19.4\%$ in Precision@5, $+15.6\%$ in NDCG@5, $+23.0\%$ in MRR, 1-tailed Wilcoxon $p = 0.0424$). Under severe adversarial stress (Tier 3), the cascade preserves a $+44.2\%$ precision advantage over flat SQL. Out-of-sample CQR validation across $N = 35{,}084$ records achieves $91.2\%$ empirical coverage for a $90.0\%$ target with $314.5\times$ dynamic interval stretching ($0.018\,\text{m}$ to $5.662\,\text{m}$), successfully grounding generative LLM synthesis in certified engineering bounds. Finally, we formalize the general mathematical protocol for translating Physics-RAG to EV battery management and wind turbine aeroelastic dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The convergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence, Large Language Models (LLMs), and Scientific Machine Learning (SciML) has unlocked transformative capabilities in automated scientific discovery, operational diagnostics, and surrogate modeling. Central to this integration is Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \cite{lewis2020rag}, which grounds model reasoning in external databases. However, directly transferring text-centric RAG paradigms to continuous, safety-critical physical systems reveals fundamental mathematical and structural pathologies.

In continuous physical dynamical systems, the primary failure mode is the \textbf{Semantic-Physical Disconnect}. In conventional language tasks, proximity within high-dimensional vector spaces correlates reliably with semantic and factual relevance. In physical systems governed by non-linear Navier-Stokes momentum fluxes, mass conservation, and threshold-dependent thermodynamic transitions, linguistic tokens and unconstrained text embeddings fail to encode dimensional similitude, regime transitions, or non-equilibrium states. 

For instance, an operational engineer querying a classical vector database for \textit{"high-energy dissipative storm scour"} may retrieve observations of a $3.5\,\text{m}$ wave breaking over a steep, reflective pocket beach ($\tan\beta \approx 0.080$, surging/plunging breakers) alongside a $3.5\,\text{m}$ wave breaking across an ultra-dissipative, macrotidal flat ($\tan\beta \approx 0.015$, spilling breakers). Although their textual descriptions are nearly identical, the nearshore dissipated wave power and subaerial geomorphic work differ by orders of magnitude. Injecting such dimensionally incompatible records into downstream neural predictors or foundation models induces severe context poisoning, precipitating unphysical hallucinations that violate fundamental conservation laws \cite{karniadakis2021physics}.

Simultaneously, indexing physical telemetry within relational databases or pure metric vector stores exposes two symmetric temporal pathologies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Chronological Blindness in Relational Stores:} Structured SQL queries execute boolean predicates efficiently (e.g., \texttt{WHERE site\_id = 'duck\_nc' AND timestamp BETWEEN ...}), but classical database engines lack hydrodynamic domain knowledge. Queries ordered by default indices select the earliest, calm onset hours of a multi-day storm window, missing the morphodynamically destructive peak by tens of hours.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Decadal Manifold Degeneracy in Metric Vector Stores:} Because fundamental fluid and thermodynamic equations are scale-invariant across time, unconstrained $k$-nearest neighbor search across continuous metric vectors retrieves sea states exhibiting identical dimensionless coordinates occurring months or decades apart, divorcing the retrieved state from its synoptic meteorological context.
    \item \textbf{Markovian Memorylessness:} Point-in-time vector embeddings discard antecedent historical context. In physical media exhibiting hysteresis, viscoelasticity, or geomorphic memory, instantaneous response is governed not merely by peak instantaneous forcing, but by the antecedent integral of cumulative stress over previous excitation cycles.
    \item \textbf{Point-Estimate Brittleness and Non-Exchangeability:} Standard neural surrogates and retrieval augmenters provide deterministic point estimates lacking rigorous uncertainty quantification. In hydro-meteorological systems, temporal autocorrelation and heteroscedastic shocks (e.g., nor'easters, hurricanes) violate independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) exchangeability \cite{barber2023conformal}. Emitting point predictions without statistically guaranteed confidence intervals induces dangerous, hallucinated precision in decision-critical zoning and civil protection.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve this impasse, this study introduces the \textbf{Two-Tier Hybrid Cascade Physics-RAG} architecture integrated with \textbf{Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR)}. We employ extreme coastal morphodynamics—specifically multi-year shoreline position evolution across contrasting global barrier environments—as our empirical proving ground. Coastal systems constitute an exceptionally demanding testbed: observational datasets are corrupted by sensor dropouts, 3D alongshore rip currents, complex wave-current interactions, and sparse in-situ surveys ($N = 1,228$ field transects across 5 years).

The primary contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physics-RAG Formulation:} We establish a generalizable methodology mapping heterogeneous sensor streams into scale-invariant physical manifolds, combining relational predicate pushdown with non-Euclidean Directional Pareto ranking.
    \item \textbf{Resolution of Dual Temporal Pathologies:} We prove empirically and statistically that cascading zero-copy columnar SQL filtering with directional manifold ranking eliminates both cross-decadal temporal drift and chronological blindness, locking onto hydrodynamic storm apexes within 0--14 hours.
    \item \textbf{Grand 100-Query Multimodal Benchmark:} We evaluate the framework on an expanded, peer-reviewed $N = 100$ query test suite spanning three operational tiers and four testing modes, proving that the Hybrid Cascade maintains superior retrieval precision and ranking fidelity even under severe adversarial ambiguity and 3D physical pathologies.
    \item \textbf{Decision-Grade Uncertainty Quantification:} We formulate a Conformalized Quantile Regression engine calibrated via multi-site stratified chronological splits, proving finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees ($\ge 90\%$) across $35{,}084$ out-of-sample records with $314.5\times$ dynamic interval stretching from quiescent swash ($1.8\,\text{cm}$) to storm apex ($5.66\,\text{m}$), and demonstrating LLM prompt grounding.
    \item \textbf{SciML Architectural Insights \& Translation:} We demonstrate the fundamental trade-off between invariant manifolds ($r = 0.574$, reaching $r = 0.839$) and PINN shock stabilization ($\text{RMSE} = 0.342\,\text{m}$), and formalize mathematical translation to EV battery degradation and wind turbine aeroelastics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Formulation: Invariant Manifolds \& Conformal Prediction}

\subsection{Generalized Continuous Physical Dynamical Systems}
Consider a general non-autonomous continuous dynamical system:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \mathbf{f}\left(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{u}(t);\, \mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}}\right), \quad \mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}(t)) + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}(t)
\label{eq:general_dynamical_system}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_x}$ represents the continuous internal state of the system, $\mathbf{u}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ is an exogenous time-varying forcing vector, $\mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_p}$ is a static physical boundary vector defining the asset, $\mathbf{y}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_y}$ represents noisy observations, and $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(t)$ denotes observational uncertainty.

In standard data-driven surrogate modeling, deep neural networks are trained directly on raw measurements $[\mathbf{u}(t), \mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}}]$. However, when the underlying operators exhibit strong non-linearities and multiscale spatial boundaries, unconstrained networks suffer from severe generalization failure out-of-distribution \cite{raissi2019pinns,lu2021deeponet}. To construct a robust retrieval and diagnostic space, we map $[\mathbf{u}(t), \mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}}]$ into a lower-dimensional, scale-invariant manifold $\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d_m}$ governed by Buckingham $\pi$-dimensional similitude \cite{buckingham1914physically}.

\subsection{Coastal Proving Ground Instantiation}
In our coastal morphodynamic proving ground, the continuous state $\mathbf{x}(t)$ is the horizontal cross-shore subaerial shoreline position $X(t)$ relative to Mean High Water (MHW), and the exogenous forcing $\mathbf{u}(t)$ comprises multi-modal oceanographic telemetries: significant wave height $H_s$, peak wave period $T_p$, wave direction $\theta_{\text{wave}}$, barometric storm surge residual $\eta_{\text{surge}}$, astronomical tide elevation $\eta_{\text{tide}}$, and 10-meter wind velocity vector $\mathbf{U}_{10}$.

\subsubsection{Static Morphological Boundary Vector}
To eliminate site-specific heuristic branches, every observatory is parameterized as an invariant point within a continuous 6-element boundary parameter space:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{\text{site}} = \left[\tan\beta,\; D_{50},\; \text{TR},\; \theta_{\text{shore}},\; Z_{\text{crest}},\; \sigma_{\text{shelter}}\right]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6
\label{eq:psite}
\end{equation}
where $\tan\beta$ is the intertidal foreshore beach slope, $D_{50}$ is median sediment grain diameter ($\text{m}$) dictating settling velocity $w_s(D_{50}, T)$ via Dietrich formulations \cite{dietrich1982settling}, $\text{TR}$ is mean spring astronomical tidal range ($\text{m}$), $\theta_{\text{shore}}$ is shore-normal azimuth (degrees relative to True North), $Z_{\text{crest}}$ is subaerial berm/dune crest elevation relative to MHW ($\text{m}$), and $\sigma_{\text{shelter}} \in [0, 1]$ is a directional wave sheltering coefficient.

\subsubsection{The Unified Kinetic Erosion Index ($\Upsilon$)}
Rather than feeding uncoupled sensor scalars to neural architectures, we formulate the Unified Kinetic Erosion Index ($\Upsilon$) as a closed-form dimensional mass-energy kinetic flux rate in SI units of $[\text{kg}/\text{s}]$:
\begin{multline}
\Upsilon = \left( \frac{\rho_w g^{\frac{3}{2}}}{16} \frac{H_s^{\frac{5}{2}} T_p}{w_s(D_{50}, T)} \right) \cdot \Psi_{\text{tide}} \cdot \Psi_{\text{wind}} \\
\cdot \left(1 + \frac{\max(0, \eta_{\text{surge}})}{H_s}\right)^2 \cdot \Phi_{\text{longshore}}
\label{eq:upsilon_master}
\end{multline}
where $\rho_w = 1025\,\text{kg/m}^3$ is seawater density, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ is gravitational acceleration, and $w_s(D_{50}, T)$ accounts for temperature-dependent kinematic seawater viscosity $\nu(T)$.

The continuous modulators in (\ref{eq:upsilon_master}) govern physical scaling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Tidal Energy Smearing ($\Psi_{\text{tide}}$):} Wave dissipation is dynamically smeared across wide intertidal sweeps via:
    \begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\text{tide}} = \left(1 + \frac{\text{TR}}{\max(0.5, H_s)}\right)^{-1}
    \label{eq:psi_tide}
    \end{equation}
    Equation~(\ref{eq:psi_tide}) smoothly modulates energy delivery across microtidal regimes (Duck, NC, $\text{TR} = 1.0\,\text{m}$, median $\Psi_{\text{tide}} \approx 0.654$) and macrotidal environments (Truc Vert, France, $\text{TR} = 4.5\,\text{m}$, median $\Psi_{\text{tide}} \approx 0.409$) without discrete boolean switching.
    \item \textbf{Onshore Wind Shear Acceleration ($\Psi_{\text{wind}}$):}
    \begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\text{wind}} = 1 + C_d \frac{\rho_a}{\rho_w} \frac{U_{10}^2 \cos(\theta_{\text{wind}} - \theta_{\text{shore}})}{g H_s}
    \label{eq:psi_wind}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Alongshore Rotational Divergence ($\Phi_{\text{longshore}}$):} Quantifies 2D embayment rotation and alongshore sediment transport gradients driven by oblique radiation stress $S_{xy}$ \cite{longuethiggins1964radiation}:
    \begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{longshore}} = 1 + \gamma_{\text{rot}} \left| \sin(2(\theta_{\text{wave}} - \theta_{\text{shore}})) \right|
    \label{eq:phi_longshore}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Scale-Invariant Dimensional Collapse}
Across global archetypes, raw baseline kinetic forcing varies by over an order of magnitude: median $\Upsilon \sim 350\,\text{kg/s}$ at Duck vs. $3{,}033\,\text{kg/s}$ at Truc Vert. To eliminate cross-site transfer bias, the index is normalized against local equilibrium carrying capacity $\Upsilon_{\text{ref, site}}$:
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon^* = \frac{\Upsilon}{\Upsilon_{\text{ref, site}}}, \quad \Delta\Upsilon^* = \frac{\Upsilon(t) - \Upsilon_{\text{eq, site}}}{\Upsilon_{\text{crit, site}}}
\label{eq:upsilon_star}
\end{equation}
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:morphodynamic_regimes}d, this normalization achieves an exact scale-invariant collapse: all four global archetypes align to a universal dimensionless distribution centered at $\Upsilon^* \approx 1.0$ (IQR $\in [0.2, 1.2]$).

\subsubsection{The 14-Dimensional State Manifold ($\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}}$)}
Each hourly observation across the multi-site golden window ($N = 175,392$ hours) is projected into a continuous 14-dimensional state vector $\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}} \in \mathbb{R}^{14}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}} = \begin{bmatrix} 
\log_{10}(\Upsilon + 1) \\ 
\log_{10}(\Omega) \\ 
\xi \\ 
\text{TWL} / Z_{\text{crest}} \\ 
\mathcal{I}_{\text{Sallenger}} \\ 
\Phi_{\text{longshore}} \\ 
S_{xx} / (\rho_w g H_s^2) \\ 
S_{xy} / (\rho_w g H_s^2) \\ 
\Psi_{\text{tide}} \\ 
\Psi_{\text{wind}} \\ 
\Delta\Upsilon^* \\ 
\Delta P_{\text{dissip}} / P_{\text{total}} \\ 
\log_{10}(E_{\text{storm\_work}} + 1) \\ 
s_0 = H_s / L_0 
\end{bmatrix}^T
\label{eq:14d_manifold}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega = H_s / (w_s T_p)$ is the dimensionless Dean number \cite{wright1984morphodynamic}, $\xi = \tan\beta / \sqrt{H_s/L_0}$ is the Iribarren surf similarity parameter \cite{battjes1974surf}, $\text{TWL} = \eta_{\text{tide}} + \eta_{\text{surge}} + R_{2\%}$ is Total Water Level incorporating wave runup \cite{stockdon2006empirical}, and $\mathcal{I}_{\text{Sallenger}} \in [1, 4]$ parameterizes Sallenger storm impact regimes (Swash, Collision, Overwash, Inundation) \cite{sallenger2000storm}.

\subsection{Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) Formulation}
To provide finite-sample, distribution-free uncertainty guarantees for continuous physical hazards, we integrate Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) \cite{romano2019conformalized,vovk2005algorithmic}. For a user-specified miscoverage rate $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ (e.g., $\alpha = 0.10$ for a $90\%$ confidence envelope), we train conditional quantile regressors $\hat{q}_{\tau}(\mathbf{x})$ targeting the lower quantile $\tau_{\text{lo}} = \alpha / 2$, median $\tau_{\text{med}} = 0.50$, and upper quantile $\tau_{\text{hi}} = 1 - \alpha / 2$.

The estimators are trained by minimizing the asymmetric pinball (check) loss \cite{koenker1978regression}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\tau(y, \hat{y}_\tau) = \max\left(\tau(y - \hat{y}_\tau),\, (\tau - 1)(y - \hat{y}_\tau)\right)
\label{eq:pinball_loss}
\end{equation}
Because the conditional quantiles depend directly on the multi-channel hydrodynamic state $\mathbf{x} = [H_s, T_p, \eta_{\text{surge}}, \dots]$, the raw interval width $\hat{q}_{\text{hi}}(\mathbf{x}) - \hat{q}_{\text{lo}}(\mathbf{x})$ naturally widens during energetic storm surges and tightens during calm swash equilibrium.

To achieve exact finite-sample validity under arbitrary non-Gaussian noise, we calibrate the model on an independent calibration partition $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{cal}}}$. We define the signed CQR non-conformity score as the point-to-boundary distance:
\begin{equation}
E_i = \max\left(\hat{q}_{\text{lo}}(\mathbf{x}_i) - y_i,\; y_i - \hat{q}_{\text{hi}}(\mathbf{x}_i)\right)
\label{eq:nonconformity_score}
\end{equation}
Notice that $E_i > 0$ when the true target $y_i$ falls outside the initial quantile envelope, and $E_i \le 0$ when $y_i$ is covered. We compute the conformal adjustment quantile:
\begin{equation}
Q_{1-\alpha} = \text{Quantile}\left(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{cal}}},\; \frac{\lceil (n_{\text{cal}} + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{n_{\text{cal}}}\right)
\label{eq:conformal_quantile}
\end{equation}
The calibrated conformal prediction band for a new query state $\mathbf{x}_{n+1}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C(\mathbf{x}_{n+1}) = \left[ \hat{q}_{\text{lo}}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1}) - Q_{1-\alpha},\; \hat{q}_{\text{hi}}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1}) + Q_{1-\alpha} \right]
\label{eq:conformal_interval}
\end{equation}
Under standard exchangeability, $C(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})$ satisfies the mathematical coverage guarantee:
\begin{equation}
P\left(Y_{n+1} \in C(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})\right) \ge 1 - \alpha
\label{eq:coverage_guarantee}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Resolving Non-Exchangeability via Stratified Chronological Slicing:} In continuous Earth observation systems, temporal autocorrelation and cross-site geomorphic shifts severely violate naive i.i.d. exchangeability \cite{barber2023conformal}. Calibrating solely on microtidal barrier beaches (Duck) and testing on macrotidal open coasts (Truc Vert) collapses empirical coverage to $32.6\%$. To guarantee valid coverage across non-stationary dynamical regimes, we implement a \textbf{multi-site stratified chronological partition}: 2012--2014 records across all four archetypes form the training split ($N = 105{,}232$), the 2015 annual sequence serves as the calibration split ($N = 35{,}076$), and the entire 2016 multi-site window is preserved as the strictly out-of-sample holdout test set ($N = 35{,}084$). This preserves temporal causality while guaranteeing cross-archetype representation.

\subsection{Tier 1: Columnar Relational Pre-Filtering (DuckDB)}
Natural language diagnostic queries contain explicit metadata constraints (e.g., \textit{"USACE FRF Duck, NC between October 2012 and March 2014"}). Metric vector stores scan the entire multi-decadal database, retrieving energetically similar analog states from irrelevant decades or geographic hemispheres. 

Tier~1 executes zero-copy predicate pushdown using DuckDB columnar Parquet scans \cite{raasveldt2019duckdb}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}} = \sigma_{\text{site\_id} = S \,\wedge\, t_{\text{start}} \le t \le t_{\text{end}} \,\wedge\, \mathbf{C}_{\text{physics}}}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{master}})
\label{eq:tier1_duckdb}
\end{equation}
By evaluating boolean predicates directly in columnar memory buffers, Tier~1 filters 175,392 hourly records in under 15 milliseconds, pruning the search space to a localized candidate subset $\mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}}$.

\subsection{Tier 2: 14D Invariant Latent Ranking via Directional Pareto Objectives}
Standard vector search calculates symmetric Euclidean distance or cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
S_C(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}) = \frac{\mathbf{z}_i \cdot \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}}{\Vert{}\mathbf{z}_i\Vert{} \Vert{}\mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}\Vert{}}
\label{eq:cosine_sim}
\end{equation}
This creates a severe physical pathology: when searching for severe hazard states (e.g., $H_s \ge 4.0\,\text{m}$), symmetric metric search penalizes an extreme $5.5\,\text{m}$ wave more heavily than a sub-threshold $3.8\,\text{m}$ wave because the $5.5\,\text{m}$ state has greater geometric distance from the target vector.

To eliminate this boundary penalty, Tier~2 introduces Directional Pareto Ranking:
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{z}_i) = (1 - \alpha) S_C(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}) \\
+ \alpha \operatorname{ReLU}\left(\frac{H_{s,i} - H_{s,\text{target}}}{H_{s,\text{target}}}\right) + \beta \max(0, \Delta\Upsilon_i^*)
\label{eq:directional_pareto}
\end{multline}
Setting $\alpha = 0.35$ and $\beta = 0.20$ converts symmetric metric distance into an asymmetric physical ranking engine that rewards candidates exceeding hazard thresholds via monotonic disequilibrium scaling.

\subsection{Continuous Dynamical Sequence Hydration ($\pm 72\text{h}$)}
Physical media exhibit extensive memory. Storm-induced damage is dictated not merely by instantaneous peak forcing, but by antecedent wave energy impulse accumulated over preceding cycles:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{impulse}} = \int_{t_0 - 72\text{h}}^{t_0} \max\left(0,\, \Upsilon(t) - \Upsilon_{\text{crit}}\right) \, dt
\label{eq:antecedent_impulse}
\end{equation}
Retrieving isolated 1-hour point embeddings leaves downstream models blind to antecedent profile draw-down. \subsection{OSSE Benchmark Formulation}
To evaluate neural architectures without observational sparsity ($N = 1,228$ discrete surveys) or unmeasured lateral fluxes, we formulated an Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE) \cite{atlas1997osse}: (i)~\textbf{Hydrodynamic Boundary Forcing}: 100\% authentic multi-year institutional sensor records (2012--2016 golden window, $N = 43,848$ contiguous hours per site) capturing authentic storm sequencing, peak wave power, and surge dynamics; and (ii)~\textbf{Closed Synthetic Nature Run}: continuous reference trajectory generated via a non-linear wave-energy equilibrium differential operator \cite{yates2009equilibrium} driven by real wave energy flux plus synthetic measurement noise ($\sigma = 0.25\,\text{m}$).

In classical data assimilation theory \cite{charney1969osse,atlas1997osse}, an OSSE nature run establishes an \emph{analytically knowable reference frame} to stress-test whether architectures accumulate compounding drift over 72-hour autonomous storm rollouts and whether asymmetric soft-penalties ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{PINN}}$) suppress unphysical fair-weather accretion during peak wave power.

\subsection{The $2 \times 2$ SciML Architectural Matrix}
We benchmarked four distinct SciML paradigms across continuous 72-hour autonomous storm rollouts and multi-year out-of-sample test sets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model A (Pure Data-Driven):} Standard MLP mapping raw inputs ($H_s, T_p, \eta_{\text{surge}}, U_{10}$) via Mean Squared Error ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}$).
    \item \textbf{Model B (Loss-Guided / PINN):} Standard inputs regularized via an asymmetric physical soft-loss penalizing seaward accretion during severe wave action:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{PINN}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_{\text{physics}} \cdot \mathbb{E}\left[\max(0, \dot{X}_{\text{pred}})^2 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\Upsilon > \Upsilon_{p80}}\right]
    \label{eq:pinn_loss}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Model C (Feature-Guided):} Unconstrained MLP supplied with our 14D invariant manifold features ($\Upsilon^*, \Omega, \xi, \text{TWL}$).
    \item \textbf{Model D (Dual-Guided):} Combines 14D invariant manifold inputs with the PINN asymmetric soft-loss penalty (\ref{eq:pinn_loss}).
\end{itemize}
