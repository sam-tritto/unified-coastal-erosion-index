\begin{abstract}
Standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and dense vector search frameworks rely on unconstrained text embeddings that frequently yield physically inconsistent retrievals when applied to continuous physical systems. In environments governed by non-linear conservation laws, linguistic proximity precipitates the \textit{Semantic-Physical Disconnect}: models retrieve states that share superficial textual descriptions but differ fundamentally in dimensional scale, phase boundaries, or non-equilibrium thresholds. Furthermore, database querying encounters a dual failure mode: relational queries suffer from \textit{chronological blindness} (defaulting to calm pre-storm onset hours rather than dynamic apexes), whereas unconstrained metric vector search suffers from \textit{cross-decadal manifold degeneracy} (matching scale-invariant dimensionless numbers across disparate epochs and losing synoptic meteorological context). Moreover, standard surrogate models output brittle point predictions lacking distribution-free uncertainty guarantees, leaving downstream foundation models susceptible to unquantified uncertainty during acute physical shocks.

To resolve these failure modes, we propose the \textbf{Two-Tier Hybrid Cascade Physics-RAG} architecture augmented with \textbf{Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR)}. Our framework projects heterogeneous multi-modal telemetries into a scale-invariant, 14-dimensional physical manifold derived from first-principles conservation laws and dimensional similitude. Retrieval is executed through a two-tier cascade: Tier~1 performs sub-15-millisecond zero-copy columnar relational pre-filtering via DuckDB to enforce spatial, temporal, and hard boundary constraints, while Tier~2 executes non-Euclidean manifold ranking governed by an asymmetric \textit{Directional Pareto} objective that prioritizes physical disequilibrium excess over symmetric geometric proximity. To combat Markovian memorylessness, the architecture hydrates continuous parent sequences ($\pm 72\text{ hours}$). Crucially, we integrate a distribution-free Conformal Prediction engine calibrated via multi-site stratified chronological splits, generating finite-sample valid prediction intervals that dynamically expand and contract with hydrograph volatility.

We evaluate this architecture across an empirical proving ground of extreme coastal morphodynamics spanning $175{,}392$ contiguous hourly hydrodynamic sensor records and $N = 1{,}228$ authentic in-situ topographic profile surveys across four contrasting global archetypes (USACE FRF Duck, NC; UNSW WRL Narrabeen, Australia; SIO Torrey Pines, CA; and Univ.~Bordeaux Truc Vert, France). On a comprehensive $N = 100$ query benchmark evaluated against deterministic physical ground-truth predicates, the Two-Tier Hybrid Cascade achieves statistically significant performance gains over both standard external baselines (Okapi BM25: $\text{P@5} = 0.052$, $p = 1.93 \times 10^{-18}$; Google Dense Embeddings: $\text{P@5} = 0.366$, $p = 0.00029$) and internal ablation variants ($\text{P@5} = 0.574$, $\text{NDCG@5} = 0.597$, $\text{MRR} = 0.598$, $\text{Regime Purity} = 0.614$). Out-of-sample CQR validation across $N = 35{,}084$ records achieves $91.2\%$ empirical coverage for a $90.0\%$ target with $314.5\times$ dynamic interval adaptation ($0.018\,\text{m}$ to $5.662\,\text{m}$). To rigorously evaluate continuous SciML surrogate rollouts without observational survey intermittency, we formulate an Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE) synthetic nature run driven by authentic multi-year boundary forcing; we demonstrate that invariant manifold features dominate multi-year correlation ($r = 0.574$, reaching $r = 0.839$) while PINN soft-loss regularization stabilizes acute storm shocks ($\text{RMSE} = 0.342\,\text{m}$), explicitly distinguishing synthetic continuous-operator verification from real-world in-situ field survey validation. Finally, we outline how these invariant-guided retrieval principles extend conceptually to other continuous cyber-physical systems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The convergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence, Large Language Models (LLMs), and Scientific Machine Learning (SciML) has unlocked transformative capabilities in automated scientific discovery, operational diagnostics, and surrogate modeling. Central to this integration is Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \cite{lewis2020rag}, which grounds model reasoning in external databases. However, directly transferring text-centric RAG paradigms to continuous, safety-critical physical systems reveals fundamental mathematical and structural pathologies.

In continuous physical dynamical systems, the primary failure mode is the \textbf{Semantic-Physical Disconnect}. In conventional language tasks, proximity within high-dimensional vector spaces correlates reliably with semantic and factual relevance. In physical systems governed by non-linear Navier-Stokes momentum fluxes, mass conservation, and threshold-dependent thermodynamic transitions, linguistic tokens and unconstrained text embeddings fail to encode dimensional similitude, regime transitions, or non-equilibrium states. 

For instance, an operational engineer querying a classical vector database for \textit{"high-energy dissipative storm scour"} may retrieve observations of a $3.5\,\text{m}$ wave breaking over a steep, reflective pocket beach ($\tan\beta \approx 0.080$, surging/plunging breakers) alongside a $3.5\,\text{m}$ wave breaking across an ultra-dissipative, macrotidal flat ($\tan\beta \approx 0.015$, spilling breakers). Although their textual descriptions are nearly identical, the nearshore dissipated wave power and subaerial geomorphic work differ by orders of magnitude. Injecting such dimensionally incompatible records into downstream neural predictors or foundation models induces severe context poisoning, precipitating unphysical hallucinations that violate fundamental conservation laws \cite{karniadakis2021physics}.

Simultaneously, indexing physical telemetry within relational databases or pure metric vector stores exposes two symmetric temporal pathologies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Chronological Blindness in Relational Stores:} Structured SQL queries execute boolean predicates efficiently (e.g., \texttt{WHERE site\_id = 'duck\_nc' AND timestamp BETWEEN ...}), but classical database engines lack hydrodynamic domain knowledge. Queries ordered by default indices select the earliest, calm onset hours of a multi-day storm window, missing the morphodynamically destructive peak by tens of hours.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Decadal Manifold Degeneracy in Metric Vector Stores:} Because fundamental fluid and thermodynamic equations are scale-invariant across time, unconstrained $k$-nearest neighbor search across continuous metric vectors retrieves sea states exhibiting identical dimensionless coordinates occurring months or decades apart, divorcing the retrieved state from its synoptic meteorological context.
    \item \textbf{Markovian Memorylessness:} Point-in-time vector embeddings discard antecedent historical context. In physical media exhibiting hysteresis, viscoelasticity, or geomorphic memory, instantaneous response is governed not merely by peak instantaneous forcing, but by the antecedent integral of cumulative stress over previous excitation cycles.
    \item \textbf{Point-Estimate Brittleness and Non-Exchangeability:} Standard neural surrogates and retrieval augmenters provide deterministic point estimates lacking rigorous uncertainty quantification. In hydro-meteorological systems, temporal autocorrelation and heteroscedastic shocks (e.g., nor'easters, hurricanes) violate independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) exchangeability \cite{barber2023conformal}. Emitting point predictions without statistically guaranteed confidence intervals induces dangerous, hallucinated precision in decision-critical zoning and civil protection.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve this impasse, this study introduces the \textbf{Two-Tier Hybrid Cascade Physics-RAG} architecture integrated with \textbf{Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR)}. We employ extreme coastal morphodynamics—specifically multi-year shoreline position evolution across contrasting global barrier environments—as our empirical proving ground. Coastal systems constitute an exceptionally demanding testbed characterized by two complementary empirical observational tiers alongside a synthetic proving ground:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continuous In-Situ Hydrodynamic Telemetry ($N = 175{,}392$ Sensor Hours):} Multi-channel, contiguous hourly records (significant wave height $H_s$, peak period $T_p$, astronomical tide $\eta_{\text{tide}}$, storm surge $\eta_{\text{surge}}$, and wind velocity $\mathbf{U}_{10}$) collected across a 5-year golden window (2012--2016) by institutional oceanographic monitoring networks.
    \item \textbf{Sparse Empirical In-Situ Topographic Surveys ($N = 1{,}228$ Field Transects):} Real-world physical beach profile surveys collected using RTK-GPS, total stations, and photogrammetry across four contrasting global archetypes: USACE FRF Duck, NC ($N = 141$ surveys) \cite{birkemeier1985users}; UNSW WRL Narrabeen, Australia ($N = 849$ surveys) \cite{turner2016multidecade}; SIO Torrey Pines, CA ($N = 110$ surveys) \cite{ludka2015beach}; and University of Bordeaux Truc Vert, France ($N = 128$ surveys) \cite{castelle2020planshape}.
    \item \textbf{Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE) Synthetic Benchmark (Fraternal Twin):} Because field surveys are separated by multi-week intervals and corrupted by unmeasured 3D rip currents, evaluating continuous 72-hour closed-loop neural network rollouts on real surveys introduces unquantifiable attribution ambiguity. Therefore, in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_osse}, we formulate a \emph{fraternal twin} OSSE nature run \cite{atlas1997osse}: continuous ground-truth shoreline trajectories generated by a non-linear equilibrium differential operator driven by 100\% authentic hydrodynamic boundary forcing with Gaussian measurement noise ($\sigma = 0.25\,\text{m}$). This explicitly decouples theoretical surrogate operator benchmarking from real-world empirical survey validation ($N = 1{,}228$).
\end{itemize}

The primary contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physics-RAG Formulation:} We establish an empirical methodology mapping heterogeneous sensor streams into scale-invariant physical manifolds, combining relational predicate pushdown with non-Euclidean Directional Pareto ranking.
    \item \textbf{Resolution of Dual Temporal Pathologies:} We prove empirically and statistically that cascading zero-copy columnar SQL filtering with directional manifold ranking eliminates both cross-decadal temporal drift and chronological blindness, locking onto hydrodynamic storm apexes within 0--14 hours.
    \item \textbf{Grand 100-Query Multimodal Benchmark with External Baselines:} We evaluate the framework on an expanded $N = 100$ query test suite evaluated against deterministic physical predicates, achieving statistically significant retrieval gains over standard dense and lexical baselines (Okapi BM25, $p < 10^{-17}$; Google Dense Embeddings, $p < 0.001$).
    \item \textbf{Decision-Grade Uncertainty Quantification:} We formulate a Conformalized Quantile Regression engine calibrated via multi-site stratified chronological splits, proving finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees ($\ge 90\%$) across $35{,}084$ out-of-sample records with $314.5\times$ dynamic interval stretching from quiescent swash ($1.8\,\text{cm}$) to storm apex ($5.66\,\text{m}$), and demonstrating LLM prompt grounding.
    \item \textbf{SciML Architectural Insights via OSSE Nature Run:} Within a synthetic Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE) nature run driven by authentic boundary forcing, we isolate the fundamental trade-off between invariant manifold representations ($r = 0.574$, reaching $r = 0.839$) and PINN soft-loss shock stabilization ($\text{RMSE} = 0.342\,\text{m}$), explicitly distinguishing synthetic closed-loop rollouts from discrete in-situ field survey validation, and outline analogous formulations for other cyber-physical domains such as battery degradation and wind turbine aerodynamics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Formulation: Invariant Manifolds \& Conformal Prediction}

\subsection{Generalized Continuous Physical Dynamical Systems}
Consider a general non-autonomous continuous dynamical system:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \mathbf{f}\left(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{u}(t);\, \mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}}\right), \quad \mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}(t)) + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}(t)
\label{eq:general_dynamical_system}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_x}$ represents the continuous internal state of the system, $\mathbf{u}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ is an exogenous time-varying forcing vector, $\mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_p}$ is a static physical boundary vector defining the asset, $\mathbf{y}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_y}$ represents noisy observations, and $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(t)$ denotes observational uncertainty.

In standard data-driven surrogate modeling, deep neural networks are trained directly on raw measurements $[\mathbf{u}(t), \mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}}]$. However, when the underlying operators exhibit strong non-linearities and multiscale spatial boundaries, unconstrained networks suffer from severe generalization failure out-of-distribution \cite{raissi2019pinns,lu2021deeponet}. To construct a robust retrieval and diagnostic space, we map $[\mathbf{u}(t), \mathbf{p}_{\text{asset}}]$ into a lower-dimensional, scale-invariant manifold $\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d_m}$ governed by Buckingham $\pi$-dimensional similitude \cite{buckingham1914physically}.

\subsection{Coastal Proving Ground Instantiation}
In our coastal morphodynamic proving ground, the continuous state $\mathbf{x}(t)$ is the horizontal cross-shore subaerial shoreline position $X(t)$ relative to Mean High Water (MHW), and the exogenous forcing $\mathbf{u}(t)$ comprises multi-modal oceanographic telemetries: significant wave height $H_s$, peak wave period $T_p$, wave direction $\theta_{\text{wave}}$, barometric storm surge residual $\eta_{\text{surge}}$, astronomical tide elevation $\eta_{\text{tide}}$, and 10-meter wind velocity vector $\mathbf{U}_{10}$.

\subsubsection{Static Morphological Boundary Vector}
To eliminate site-specific heuristic branches, every observatory is parameterized as an invariant point within a continuous 6-element boundary parameter space:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{\text{site}} = \left[\tan\beta,\; D_{50},\; \text{TR},\; \theta_{\text{shore}},\; Z_{\text{crest}},\; \sigma_{\text{shelter}}\right]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6
\label{eq:psite}
\end{equation}
where $\tan\beta$ is the intertidal foreshore beach slope, $D_{50}$ is median sediment grain diameter ($\text{m}$) dictating settling velocity $w_s(D_{50}, T)$ via Dietrich formulations \cite{dietrich1982settling}, $\text{TR}$ is mean spring astronomical tidal range ($\text{m}$), $\theta_{\text{shore}}$ is shore-normal azimuth (degrees relative to True North), $Z_{\text{crest}}$ is subaerial berm/dune crest elevation relative to MHW ($\text{m}$), and $\sigma_{\text{shelter}} \in [0, 1]$ is a directional wave sheltering coefficient.

\subsubsection{The Unified Kinetic Erosion Index ($\Upsilon$)}
Rather than feeding uncoupled sensor scalars to neural architectures, we formulate the Unified Kinetic Erosion Index ($\Upsilon$, designated across operational frameworks and software distributions as the Unified Coastal Erosion Index or Unified Coastal Vulnerability Index) as a closed-form dimensional mass-energy kinetic flux rate in SI units of $[\text{kg}/\text{s}]$:
\begin{multline}
\Upsilon = \left( \frac{\rho_w g^{\frac{3}{2}}}{16 (2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}} \tan\beta \frac{H_s^n T_p}{w_s(D_{50}, T)} \right) \\
\cdot \left[1 + \frac{\max(0, \eta_{\text{surge}})}{s_{\text{ref}}} + \frac{\max(0, R_{2\%})}{r_{\text{ref}}}\right] \\
\cdot \Psi_{\text{tide}} \cdot \Psi_{\text{phase}} \cdot \Psi_{\text{dir}} \cdot \Psi_{\text{wind}}
\label{eq:upsilon_master}
\end{multline}
where $\rho_w = 1025\,\text{kg/m}^3$ is seawater density, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ is gravitational acceleration, $\tan\beta$ is the intertidal foreshore slope, and $w_s(D_{50}, T)$ accounts for temperature-dependent kinematic seawater viscosity $\nu(T)$ and dynamic sediment fall velocity \cite{dietrich1982settling,soulsby1997dynamics}. Linear wave theory establishes the dimensional prefactor $C_0 = \frac{\rho_w g^{3/2}}{16(2\pi)^{3/2}} \approx 124.96\,\text{kg}/(\text{m}^{1.5}\cdot\text{s}^3)$ (or $199.11\,\text{kg}/(\text{m}^{1.5}\cdot\text{s}^3)$ when referenced to submerged sediment density $\rho_s - \rho_w$, detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_modulators}) from the deepwater group velocity $c_g = g T_p / (4\pi)$ and wave energy density $E = \frac{1}{16}\rho_w g H_s^2$. At the baseline kinematic exponent $n = 2.5$, the product $C_0 \tan\beta (H_s^{2.5} T_p / w_s)$ yields dimensions of $[\text{kg}/\text{s}]$, representing the instantaneous rate of kinetic sediment-forcing work delivered to the subaerial beach face.

The continuous dimensionless modulators in (\ref{eq:upsilon_master}) govern physical regimes (with complete definitions and asymptotic limits detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_modulators}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Tidal Energy Smearing ($\Psi_{\text{tide}}$):} Wave dissipation is dynamically smeared across wide intertidal sweeps via:
    \begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\text{tide}} = \left(1 + \frac{\text{TR}}{2 \max(0.5, H_s)}\right)^{-1}
    \label{eq:psi_tide}
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{TR}/2$ is the astronomical tidal amplitude. The floor $\max(0.5, H_s)$ eliminates division-by-zero singularities during calm conditions, while a strict numerical saturation ceiling ($\Psi_{\text{tide}} \le 1.0$, and for instantaneous excursions $\Psi_{\text{phase}} \le 10.0$ or exponential inundation modulators $\operatorname{clip}(\exp((\eta - \text{MHW})/\max(0.5, H_s)), 0.1, 10.0)$) prevents unbounded exponential magnification during extreme hurricane surges or calm spring tides. Equation~(\ref{eq:psi_tide}) smoothly modulates energy delivery across microtidal regimes (Duck, NC, $\text{TR} = 1.0\,\text{m}$, median $\Psi_{\text{tide}} \approx 0.654$) and macrotidal environments (Truc Vert, France, $\text{TR} = 4.5\,\text{m}$, median $\Psi_{\text{tide}} \approx 0.409$) without heuristic branching.
    \item \textbf{Instantaneous High-Tide Wave Energy Gating ($\Psi_{\text{phase}}$):} Modulates hourly wave attack by water level elevation relative to the berm:
    \begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\text{phase}} = \left[\frac{\eta_{\text{tide}} - \text{MSL}}{\text{TR}/2} + 1\right]^p
    \label{eq:psi_phase}
    \end{equation}
    concentrating breaking wave power against the dune toe at high tide ($\Psi_{\text{phase}} \to 2^p$) while extinguishing upper-beach attack at low tide ($\Psi_{\text{phase}} \to 0$), eliminating false-positive erosion signals during offshore intertidal dissipation.
    \item \textbf{Rectified Shore-Normal Directional Alignment ($\Psi_{\text{dir}}$):}
    \begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\text{dir}} = \max\left(0,\, \cos(\theta_{\text{wave}} - \theta_{\text{shore}})\right)^2
    \label{eq:psi_dir}
    \end{equation}
    matching shore-normal radiation stress $S_{xx} \propto E \cos^2\theta_{\text{rel}}$ governing cross-shore gouging, while rectifying shadowed waves ($|\theta_{\text{rel}}| \ge 90^\circ$) to zero.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Onshore/Offshore Wind Stress ($\Psi_{\text{wind}}$):} Onshore winds ($W_a \ge 0$) enhance setup and overtopping, whereas offshore winds ($W_a < 0$) flatten wave steepness:
    \begin{equation}
    \Psi_{\text{wind}} = \begin{cases} 
    1 + \min\left(c_{\text{on}} (W_a / W_{\text{ref}})^2,\, w_{b,\text{on}}\right), & W_a \ge 0 \\
    1 - \min\left(c_{\text{off}} (|W_a| / W_{\text{ref}})^2,\, w_{b,\text{off}}\right), & W_a < 0
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:psi_wind}
    \end{equation}
    with $W_a = U_{10} \cos(\theta_{\text{wind}} - \theta_{\text{shore}})$, $c_{\text{on}} = 1.0$, and $c_{\text{off}} = 0.5$.
    \item \textbf{Coupled 2D Embayment Alongshore Divergence ($\Phi_{\text{longshore}}$):}
    \begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{longshore}} = \sin\left(2(\theta_{\text{wave}} - \theta_{\text{shore}})\right)
    \label{eq:phi_longshore}
    \end{equation}
    Quantifies alongshore sediment transport gradients driven by oblique radiation stress $S_{xy}$ \cite{longuethiggins1964radiation}. Oblique storms drive headland beach rotation across bounded embayments via $\Delta X_{\text{net}} = \Delta X_{\text{cross}}(\Upsilon) + \alpha_{\text{rot}} \Phi_{\text{longshore}} \left(\frac{y - y_{\text{center}}}{L_{\text{beach}}}\right)$. Rather than altering scalar 1D cross-shore flux, $\Phi_{\text{longshore}}$ enters the 14D invariant manifold vector $\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}}$ to inform retrieval and 2D spatial forecasting.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Scale-Invariant Dimensional Collapse}
Across global archetypes, raw baseline kinetic forcing varies by over an order of magnitude: median $\Upsilon \sim 350\,\text{kg/s}$ at Duck vs. $3{,}033\,\text{kg/s}$ at Truc Vert. To eliminate cross-site transfer bias, the index is normalized against local equilibrium carrying capacity $\Upsilon_{\text{ref, site}}$:
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon^* = \frac{\Upsilon}{\Upsilon_{\text{ref, site}}}, \quad \Delta\Upsilon^* = \frac{\Upsilon(t) - \Upsilon_{\text{eq, site}}}{\Upsilon_{\text{crit, site}}}
\label{eq:upsilon_star}
\end{equation}
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:morphodynamic_regimes}d, this normalization achieves an exact scale-invariant collapse: all four global archetypes align to a universal dimensionless distribution centered at $\Upsilon^* \approx 1.0$ (IQR $\in [0.2, 1.2]$).

\subsubsection{The 14-Dimensional State Manifold ($\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}}$)}
Each hourly observation across the multi-site golden window ($N = 175,392$ hours) is projected into a continuous 14-dimensional state vector $\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}} \in \mathbb{R}^{14}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}} = \begin{bmatrix} 
\log_{10}(\Upsilon + 1) \\ 
\log_{10}(\Omega) \\ 
\xi \\ 
\text{TWL} / Z_{\text{crest}} \\ 
\mathcal{I}_{\text{Sallenger}} \\ 
\Phi_{\text{longshore}} \\ 
S_{xx} / (\rho_w g H_s^2) \\ 
S_{xy} / (\rho_w g H_s^2) \\ 
\Psi_{\text{tide}} \\ 
\Psi_{\text{wind}} \\ 
\Delta\Upsilon^* \\ 
\Delta P_{\text{dissip}} / P_{\text{total}} \\ 
\log_{10}(E_{\text{storm\_work}} + 1) \\ 
s_0 = H_s / L_0 
\end{bmatrix}^T
\label{eq:14d_manifold}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega = H_s / (w_s T_p)$ is the dimensionless Dean number \cite{wright1984morphodynamic}, $\xi = \tan\beta / \sqrt{H_s/L_0}$ is the Iribarren surf similarity parameter \cite{battjes1974surf}, $\text{TWL} = \eta_{\text{tide}} + \eta_{\text{surge}} + R_{2\%}$ is Total Water Level incorporating wave runup \cite{stockdon2006empirical}, and $\mathcal{I}_{\text{Sallenger}} \in [1, 4]$ parameterizes Sallenger storm impact regimes (Swash, Collision, Overwash, Inundation) \cite{sallenger2000storm}.

\subsection{Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) Formulation}
To provide finite-sample, distribution-free uncertainty guarantees for continuous physical hazards, we integrate Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,romano2019conformalized,angelopoulos2021gentle}. For a user-specified miscoverage rate $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ (e.g., $\alpha = 0.10$ for a $90\%$ confidence envelope), we train conditional quantile regressors $\hat{q}_{\tau}(\mathbf{x})$ targeting the lower quantile $\tau_{\text{lo}} = \alpha / 2$, median $\tau_{\text{med}} = 0.50$, and upper quantile $\tau_{\text{hi}} = 1 - \alpha / 2$.

The estimators are trained by minimizing the asymmetric pinball (check) loss \cite{koenker1978regression}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\tau(y, \hat{y}_\tau) = \max\left(\tau(y - \hat{y}_\tau),\, (\tau - 1)(y - \hat{y}_\tau)\right)
\label{eq:pinball_loss}
\end{equation}
Because the conditional quantiles depend directly on the multi-channel hydrodynamic state $\mathbf{x} = [H_s, T_p, \eta_{\text{surge}}, \dots]$, the raw interval width $\hat{q}_{\text{hi}}(\mathbf{x}) - \hat{q}_{\text{lo}}(\mathbf{x})$ naturally widens during energetic storm surges and tightens during calm swash equilibrium.

To achieve exact finite-sample validity under arbitrary non-Gaussian noise, we calibrate the model on an independent calibration partition $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{cal}}}$. We define the signed CQR non-conformity score as the point-to-boundary distance:
\begin{equation}
E_i = \max\left(\hat{q}_{\text{lo}}(\mathbf{x}_i) - y_i,\; y_i - \hat{q}_{\text{hi}}(\mathbf{x}_i)\right)
\label{eq:nonconformity_score}
\end{equation}
Notice that $E_i > 0$ when the true target $y_i$ falls outside the initial quantile envelope, and $E_i \le 0$ when $y_i$ is covered. We compute the conformal adjustment quantile:
\begin{equation}
Q_{1-\alpha} = \text{Quantile}\left(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{cal}}},\; \frac{\lceil (n_{\text{cal}} + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{n_{\text{cal}}}\right)
\label{eq:conformal_quantile}
\end{equation}
The calibrated conformal prediction band for a new query state $\mathbf{x}_{n+1}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C(\mathbf{x}_{n+1}) = \left[ \hat{q}_{\text{lo}}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1}) - Q_{1-\alpha},\; \hat{q}_{\text{hi}}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1}) + Q_{1-\alpha} \right]
\label{eq:conformal_interval}
\end{equation}
Under standard exchangeability, $C(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})$ satisfies the mathematical coverage guarantee:
\begin{equation}
P\left(Y_{n+1} \in C(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})\right) \ge 1 - \alpha
\label{eq:coverage_guarantee}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Resolving Non-Exchangeability via Stratified Chronological Slicing:} In continuous Earth observation systems, temporal autocorrelation and cross-site geomorphic shifts severely violate naive i.i.d. exchangeability \cite{barber2023conformal}. Calibrating solely on microtidal barrier beaches (Duck) and testing on macrotidal open coasts (Truc Vert) collapses empirical coverage to $32.6\%$. To guarantee valid coverage across non-stationary dynamical regimes, we implement a \textbf{multi-site stratified chronological partition}: 2012--2014 records across all four archetypes form the training split ($N = 105{,}232$), the 2015 annual sequence serves as the calibration split ($N = 35{,}076$), and the entire 2016 multi-site window is preserved as the strictly out-of-sample holdout test set ($N = 35{,}084$). This preserves temporal causality while guaranteeing cross-archetype representation.

\section{Architecture: Two-Tier Hybrid Cascade Physics-RAG}

Natural language diagnostic queries contain explicit metadata constraints (e.g., \textit{"USACE FRF Duck, NC between October 2012 and March 2014"}). \mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}} = \sigma_{\text{site\_id} = S \,\wedge\, t_{\text{start}} \le t \le t_{\text{end}} \,\wedge\, \mathbf{C}_{\text{physics}}}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{master}})

\end{equation}
By evaluating boolean predicates directly in columnar memory buffers, Tier~1 filters 175,392 hourly records in under 15 milliseconds, pruning the search space to a localized candidate subset $\mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}}$.

\subsection{Tier 2: 14D Invariant Latent Ranking via Directional Pareto Objectives}
Standard vector search calculates symmetric Euclidean distance or cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
S_C(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}) = \frac{\mathbf{z}_i \cdot \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}}{\Vert{}\mathbf{z}_i\Vert{} \Vert{}\mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}\Vert{}}
\label{eq:cosine_sim}
\end{equation}
This creates a severe physical pathology: when searching for severe hazard states (e.g., $H_s \ge 4.0\,\text{m}$), symmetric metric search penalizes an extreme $5.5\,\text{m}$ wave more heavily than a sub-threshold $3.8\,\text{m}$ wave because the $5.5\,\text{m}$ state has greater geometric distance from the target vector.

To eliminate this boundary penalty while guaranteeing that Pareto excess cannot asymptotically overpower bounded metric similarity ($S_C \in [-1, 1]$) under extreme storm shocks ($H_{s, i} \gg H_{s,\text{target}}$), Tier~2 introduces Directional Pareto Ranking with a saturating sub-linear activation via the hyperbolic tangent:
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{z}_i) = (1 - \alpha) S_C(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}) \\
+ \alpha \tanh\left(\left[\frac{H_{s,i} - H_{s,\text{target}}}{H_{s,\text{target}}}\right]_+\right) + \beta \tanh(\max(0, \Delta\Upsilon_i^*))
\label{eq:directional_pareto}
\end{multline}
where $[x]_+ = \max(0, x)$. Setting $\alpha = 0.35$ and $\beta = 0.20$ converts symmetric metric distance into an asymmetric physical ranking engine that rewards candidates exceeding hazard thresholds: for moderate excesses ($x \ll 1$), $\tanh(x) \approx x$ preserves the linear Pareto gradient, while for catastrophic storm shocks ($x \gg 1$), $\tanh(x) \to 1.0$ smoothly saturates into $[0, 1)$, preventing unbounded wave height spikes from overpowering the 14D manifold geometry.

\subsection{Bayesian Invariant Baseline Diagnostics: Heavy Tails ($k > 0.5$) and Motivation for CQR}
\label{sec:bayesian_loo_breakdown}
Prior to formulating distribution-free Conformal Prediction, we benchmarked parametric Bayesian formulations of the unified index ($\Upsilon$) using Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling via the No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS) to estimate parameter posteriors and evaluate out-of-sample predictive accuracy via Pareto-Smoothed Importance Sampling Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (PSIS-LOO-CV) \cite{vehtari2017practical}. While Bayesian $\Upsilon$ baselines achieve stable chain convergence under modal wave conditions ($\hat{R} < 1.05$, effective sample sizes $\text{ESS} > 1{,}000$), ArviZ PSIS-LOO diagnostics systematically flag estimated Pareto shape parameters exceeding the reliability threshold ($k > 0.5$, with peak values $k > 0.7$) during extreme nor'easter cutoffs and severe hurricane storm-surge spikes.

In PSIS diagnostic theory \cite{vehtari2017practical}, a Pareto shape $k > 0.5$ signals that the importance sampling weights possess high or infinite variance, indicating extreme sensitivity of the posterior to individual high-leverage observations (e.g., rapid episodic scarping under catastrophic wave power). Attempting to enforce parametric normality or Student-$t$ likelihoods under such non-stationary hydrodynamic impulses distorts parameter estimates and leads to erratic credible interval widths. This heavy-tail diagnostic breakdown scientifically justifies transitioning away from parametric Bayesian inference to non-parametric, distribution-free Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR), as developed in Section~\ref{sec:conformal_uq}, to provide mathematically certified, finite-sample coverage guarantees for decision-grade coastal hazard warnings.

\section{Discussion, SciML Design Principles \& Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Deploying generative foundation models to continuous physical systems requires anchoring retrieval and reasoning in first-principles conservation laws. Our findings establish four general SciML design principles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Invariant Manifolds Outperform Loss Regularizers in Continuous OSSE Benchmarks:} Within the controlled OSSE synthetic nature run (Section~\ref{sec:appendix_osse}), feature-guided invariant projections (Model C) achieve superior multi-year tracking ($r = 0.574$, reaching $r = 0.839$) compared to unguided networks ($r = 0.539$) and PINN soft constraints ($r = 0.533$). While PINN soft-loss penalties stabilize acute shocks during peak storm hours ($\text{RMSE} = 0.342\,\text{m}$), pre-computed physical invariants establish exact coordinate mappings that prevent optimization stiffness over multi-year rollout horizons.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Cascades Resolve Dual Temporal Pathologies:} Physical time series require a two-tier cascade: zero-copy relational SQL pre-filtering eliminates cross-decadal temporal drift, while asymmetric Directional Pareto ranking eliminates chronological blindness ($P_{\text{apex}}@5 = 0.6078$). Across $N = 100$ queries, the Hybrid Cascade achieves statistically significant gains over Pure Physics-RAG ($p = 0.0253$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[+0.0020, +0.1120]$) and preserves robust competency under adversarial tests ($P@5 = 0.3100$) where SQL collapses ($0.2150$).
    \item \textbf{Sequence Hydration Combats Markovian Memorylessness:} Point-in-time vector embeddings discard antecedent energy impulses required to predict threshold damage. Pointer hydration ($\pm 72\text{h}$) restores temporal continuity, enabling grounded, physically consistent failure diagnosis across field anomalies.
    \item \textbf{Conformal Prediction Guarantees Distribution-Free Safety Bounds:} Point predictions in dynamical systems provide false certainty during extreme shocks. Multi-site stratified CQR guarantees finite-sample marginal coverage ($91.2\%$ out-of-sample coverage across $35{,}084$ records) with $314.5\times$ dynamic interval adaptation, transforming generative LLM prompts into certified civil defense tools.
\end{enumerate}

By combining zero-copy columnar DuckDB, vectorized latent neighbors, and calibrated CQR quantile regressors, the entire system executes in under 15 milliseconds on commodity hardware, providing an open-source, scalable blueprint for operational hazard monitoring and forecasting across continuous cyber-physical systems.

\section{Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE) Framework \& SciML Architectural Ablations}
\label{sec:appendix_osse}

\subsection{OSSE Benchmark Formulation: The Fraternal Twin Framework}
To evaluate neural architectures without observational sparsity ($N = 1,228$ discrete surveys) or unmeasured lateral fluxes, we formulated an Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE) \cite{atlas1997osse}: (i)~\textbf{Hydrodynamic Boundary Forcing}: 100\% authentic multi-year institutional sensor records (2012--2016 golden window, $N = 43,848$ contiguous hours per site) capturing authentic storm sequencing, peak wave power, and surge dynamics; and (ii)~\textbf{Closed Synthetic Nature Run}: continuous reference trajectory generated via a non-linear wave-energy equilibrium differential operator \cite{yates2009equilibrium} driven by real wave energy flux plus synthetic measurement noise ($\sigma = 0.25\,\text{m}$).

In geoscientific data assimilation, an ``identical twin'' experiment occurs when evaluated neural architectures share identical differential equations or forward assumptions with the nature run generator, leading to circular validation. To ensure methodological rigor, this benchmark is strictly constructed as a \emph{fraternal twin experiment} under three distinct safeguards:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Noise \& Authentic Asymmetry:} The nature run incorporates additive stochastic Gaussian observation noise ($\sigma = 0.25\,\text{m}$), unmeasured antecedent memory decay, and authentic asymmetric multi-site hydrodynamic boundary forcing from institutional oceanographic buoys.
    \item \textbf{Non-ODE Neural Surrogates:} The candidate neural architectures (LSTMs and MLPs) possess no structural or architectural knowledge of the underlying ODE. They are unconstrained neural parameterizations that must discover temporal integration mechanics, seasonal hysteresis, and non-linear physical interactions purely from sequential training data.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled External Empirical Validation:} Continuous 72-hour closed-loop rollouts are restricted to theoretical operator stress-testing; external, non-identical empirical validation is independently provided by the $N = 1{,}228$ real-world in-situ field profile surveys at Duck, NC and across all four global archetypes (Section~IV).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Algebraic Derivation, Sediment Buoyancy, and Dimensional Consistency of Prefactor $C_0$}
The master formulation for $\Upsilon_0$ couples wave power dissipation with cross-shore sediment transport kinetics. In deep water, linear wave theory establishes the omnidirectional wave energy flux (power per unit crest length) as:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{deep}} = E c_g = \left(\frac{1}{16}\rho_w g H_s^2\right) \left(\frac{g T_p}{4\pi}\right) = \frac{\rho_w g^2}{64\pi} H_s^2 T_p
\end{equation}
As waves shoal onto an intertidal beach profile of slope $\tan\beta$, nearshore radiation stress $S_{xx} = \frac{3}{16}\rho_w g H_s^2$ drives intense turbulent dissipation and suspended sediment mobilization. According to Dean's equilibrium beach profile mechanics \cite{dean1973heuristic}, the rate of sediment-forcing work scales inversely with sediment settling velocity $w_s(D_{50}, T)$ and directly with foreshore gradient $\tan\beta$. Non-dimensionalizing the wave period via deepwater wavelength $L_0 = \frac{g T_p^2}{2\pi}$ and normalizing the group velocity across the dynamic breaker zone $c_b \approx \sqrt{g H_s}$ yields the instantaneous mass flux rate:
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon_0 = C_0 \tan\beta \frac{H_s^{5/2} T_p}{w_s(D_{50}, T)}, \quad C_0 = \frac{\rho_w g^{3/2}}{16(2\pi)^{3/2}}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{1. Fluid vs. Submerged Sediment Density Formulations:}
Evaluating $C_0$ using standard seawater fluid density $\rho_w = 1025\,\text{kg/m}^3$ and gravitational acceleration $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ yields:
\begin{align}
C_0 &= \frac{1025 \times 9.81^{3/2}}{16 (2\pi)^{3/2}} = \frac{1025 \times 30.7205}{16 \times 15.7496} \nonumber \\
    &\approx 124.96\,\text{kg}/(\text{m}^{1.5}\cdot\text{s}^3)
\end{align}
This is the exact numerical constant evaluated in \texttt{src/physics.py}. Alternatively, if the prefactor explicitly incorporates the submerged sediment density $\Delta\rho = \rho_s - \rho_w = (s - 1)\rho_w$ (with quartz sand $\rho_s \approx 2658\,\text{kg/m}^3$, relative submerged density $s - 1 = \frac{\rho_s - \rho_w}{\rho_w} \approx 1.593$, and $\Delta\rho \approx 1633\,\text{kg/m}^3$):
\begin{align}
C_{0,\text{sed}} &= \frac{(\rho_s - \rho_w) g^{3/2}}{16(2\pi)^{3/2}} = (s - 1) C_0 \nonumber \\
                 &= \frac{1633 \times 30.7205}{16 \times 15.7496} \approx 199.11\,\text{kg}/(\text{m}^{1.5}\cdot\text{s}^3)
\end{align}
Both formulations share identical physical dimensions:
\begin{align}
[C_0] &= [M][L]^{-3} \cdot ([L][T]^{-2})^{1.5} \nonumber \\
      &= [M][L]^{-1.5}[T]^{-3} \implies \text{kg}/(\text{m}^{1.5}\cdot\text{s}^3)
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{2. Dimensional Verification with Dynamic Settling Velocity $w_s$:}
Rather than normalizing by a static reference velocity $w_{s,\text{ref}}$, the wave-sediment core $\mathcal{K}_{\text{core}} = \tan\beta \frac{H_s^{2.5} T_p}{w_s(D_{50}, T)}$ dynamically incorporates Dietrich/Soulsby settling kinetics \cite{dietrich1982settling,soulsby1997dynamics}, capturing seasonal water temperature viscosity $\nu(T)$ and grain size $D_{50}$ in $[\text{m}/\text{s}]$. Multiplying $C_0$ by $\mathcal{K}_{\text{core}}$:
\begin{equation}
[\Upsilon_0] = \left[\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^{1.5}\cdot\text{s}^3}\right] \times [-] \times \frac{[\text{m}^{2.5}][\text{s}]}{[\text{m}/\text{s}]} = [\text{kg}/\text{s}]
\end{equation}
confirming dimensional consistency as an instantaneous sediment-forcing mass flux rate $[\text{kg}/\text{s}]$.

\noindent\textbf{3. Reconciliation with Historical Draft Notes ($199.1$ and $72.82$):}
In earlier preliminary working notes, an alternate value of $72.82$ was cited based on deepwater wave power $P_{\text{deep}} = \frac{\rho g^2}{64\pi}$ divided by a shallow-water wave celerity scale $\sqrt{g}$, while the value $199.1\,\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-1/2}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$ was noted when substituting submerged sediment density $\Delta\rho = 1633\,\text{kg/m}^3$ under an alternative non-dimensionalization of settling velocity. The operational codebase (\texttt{src/physics.py}) rigorously standardizes on $C_0 = \frac{\rho_w g^{3/2}}{16(2\pi)^{3/2}} \approx 124.96\,\text{kg}/(\text{m}^{1.5}\cdot\text{s}^3)$.

Crucially, because scale-invariant dimensional collapse normalizes $\Upsilon(t)$ by the site reference capacity $\Upsilon_{\text{ref, site}} \propto C_0$:
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon^*(t) = \frac{\Upsilon(t)}{\Upsilon_{\text{ref, site}}} = \frac{C_0 \tan\beta \frac{H_s^{2.5} T_p}{w_s} \prod \Psi_k}{C_0 \tan\beta_{\text{site}} \frac{\overline{H}_{s,\text{site}}^{2.5} \overline{T}_{p,\text{site}}}{w_{s,\text{site}}}}
\end{equation}
any constant scalar factor in $C_0$ cancels out identically in the numerator and denominator ($\Upsilon^*(t) \equiv \Upsilon_0(t) / \Upsilon_{0,\text{ref}}$). Consequently, the 14D invariant manifold $\mathbf{z}_{\text{physics}}$, the Directional Pareto ranking, and all downstream retrieval scores $\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{z}_i)$ are mathematically and numerically invariant to the choice between $124.96$ and $199.11$.
